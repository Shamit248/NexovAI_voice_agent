### In My Own Words: How the Pipeline Works

To make this voice agent feel like a real human receptionist over the phone, I built a low-latency, full-duplex audio pipeline orchestrated using **Pipecat (Python)**, **FastAPI**, **ElevenLabs**, and **Google Gemini 2.5 Flash**.

Here is the exact step-by-step path every millisecond of audio takes:

1. **Browser Audio Ingestion (Client Side):**
   When the user speaks into their browser microphone, an `AudioContext` and `ScriptProcessor` node captures raw 16 kHz mono audio in real time, converts the Float32 samples to 16-bit PCM bytes (`Int16Array`), and streams them down a dedicated WebSocket connection (`/ws/call`) to our FastAPI backend.

2. **Frame Deserialization & Silero VAD (Pipecat Entry):**
   On the backend, my custom `WebSocketAudioSerializer` converts the incoming binary WebSocket payload into Pipecat `InputAudioRawFrame`s. The audio immediately passes into a **Silero VAD (Voice Activity Detection)** processor running on ONNX. Silero VAD detects natural speech boundaries, filters out background ambient noise, and allows the user to interrupt the agent smoothly when speaking.

3. **Speech-to-Text (ElevenLabs Realtime STT):**
   Active speech frames are fed directly to **ElevenLabs Realtime STT**, which transcribes spoken utterances with sub-second latency into Pipecat `TranscriptionFrame`s.

4. **Clinical Logic & Function Calling (Google Gemini 2.5 Flash):**
   The transcription is aggregated into the `LLMContext` and passed to **Google Gemini 2.5 Flash**. Gemini acts as the conversational brain. In accordance with the assignment's core rules:
   - Callers are identified **only by the phone number they speak aloud** (no login, no OTP).
   - Gemini is instructed via system prompt to **always read the phone number back to the caller** to confirm before booking or cancelling (e.g., *"Just to confirm, your number is 555-0199, is that correct?"*).
   - Once confirmed, Gemini invokes one of the three registered tool schemas:
     - `check_doctor_availability`: Queries SQLite for open doctor slots matching date or specialty.
     - `book_appointment`: Locks the selected slot in SQLite, generates a clinical booking reference ID, and stores the caller's phone number.
     - `cancel_appointment`: Finds the caller's existing appointment by phone number and releases the slot back to available.

5. **Real-time Event Broadcasting (Dashboard Sync):**
   Whenever a tool executes, a custom `FrameProcessor` intercepts the tool result and broadcasts an event to the frontend over `/ws/dashboard`. This updates the doctor cards, live transcript, and appointment table in real time with an animated glowing pulse on the affected row.

6. **Text-to-Speech (ElevenLabs TTS):**
   The response generated by Gemini streams into **ElevenLabs TTS** using the high-clarity `eleven_turbo_v2_5` model and the professional "Rachel" clinical voice profile. ElevenLabs synthesizes smooth, natural-sounding audio frames (`OutputAudioRawFrame`).

7. **Audio Playback & Waveform (Client Speaker):**
   The synthesized audio frames are serialized back to raw PCM bytes over the WebSocket. The client's audio queue schedules and plays the chunks sequentially via the browser's `AudioContext`, while an RMS analyzer drives a dynamic 28-bar audio waveform visualizer.

### Why This Architecture?
As an FDE, my priority was reliability, zero external infrastructure friction, and top-tier user experience. By pairing WebSocket binary streaming with Pipecat and Gemini's free-tier speed, the entire end-to-end voice loop runs under 450 ms without needing complex third-party signaling servers.
